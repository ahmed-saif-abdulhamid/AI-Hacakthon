# Phase 2, step 1: Lean Canvas

Variant: Late-Payment Follow-Up Toolkit

Each statement cites verified claim IDs or is labelled ASSUMPTION by code.

## Problem

- ASSUMPTION: Freelancers waste time manually chasing late payments and lack a systematic follow-up process.
- Existing invoicing tools focus on creation, not on structured reminder sequences for overdue invoices.[C21]

## Customer Segments

- ASSUMPTION: Freelance developers and designers who manage their own invoicing and collections.

## Unique Value Proposition

- ASSUMPTION: A ready-to-use toolkit of templates and checklists that standardizes late-payment follow-up without new software.

## Solution

- ASSUMPTION: Downloadable templates, checklists, and guides for escalating reminder sequences and payment tracking.

## Channels

- ASSUMPTION: Founder's professional network, freelance communities, and SEO content on late-payment workflows.

## Revenue Streams

- ASSUMPTION: One-time purchase of the digital toolkit with optional future updates.

## Cost Structure

- ASSUMPTION: Hosting, payment processing, and content creation within a $300 budget.

## Key Metrics

- ASSUMPTION: Waitlist sign-ups and conversion rate to paid toolkit purchases.

## Unfair Advantage

- ASSUMPTION: Founder's 7 years of freelance web development experience and documented follow-up workflows.

## Riskiest assumption

- ASSUMPTION: Freelancers will pay for a document-based toolkit instead of adopting automated invoicing software.

## Experiment

- ASSUMPTION: Launch a waitlist landing page describing the toolkit's templates and checklists; measure sign-ups and pre-sale commitments.

Pre-registered demand target: 100 waitlist sign-ups in 14 days (team default, ASSUMPTION).
